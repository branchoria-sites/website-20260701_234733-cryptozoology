## Introduction

The strongest genetic evidence ever gathered from alleged yeti [relics]({{ 'relics/' | relative_url }}) has not pointed to an unknown ape, surviving hominin, or undiscovered Himalayan mammal. Instead, DNA studies of purported yeti hair, bones, teeth, skin fragments and faecal [samples]({{ 'samples/' | relative_url }}) have repeatedly identified known bear species from the Himalayas and Tibetan Plateau. The most influential investigations transformed a long-running cryptozoological question into a testable biological one: what animal actually produced the relics preserved in monasteries, museums and private collections? The answer, in nearly every well-documented case, was a regional bear. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/284/1868/20171804/78775/Evolutionary-history-of-enigmatic-bears-in-the" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royalsocietypublishing.org">[Royal Society Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royalsocietypublishing.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Society PublishingEvolutionary history of enigmatic bears in the Tibetan Plateau...Nov 29, 2017 — Recently, two purported yeti sam...</span></span></span>

These findings did more than challenge yeti claims. They also provided new information about the evolutionary history of Himalayan and Tibetan bears, showing how cryptid-labelled [specimens]({{ 'specimens/' | relative_url }}) can contribute to mainstream zoological research when chain of custody and laboratory methods are carefully documented. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/284/1868/20171804/78775/Evolutionary-history-of-enigmatic-bears-in-the" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royalsocietypublishing.org">[Royal Society Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royalsocietypublishing.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Society PublishingEvolutionary history of enigmatic bears in the Tibetan Plateau...Nov 29, 2017 — Recently, two purported yeti sam...</span></span></span>

## What Kinds of Relics Were Tested?

For decades, alleged yeti evidence included a wide variety of biological materials collected across Nepal, Bhutan, Tibet and northern India. These relics ranged from loose hairs and skin fragments to bones, teeth and preserved body parts displayed as cultural or religious objects.

A major turning point came when researchers assembled specimens from museums, monasteries and private collections rather than relying solely on newly collected field samples. In the most comprehensive study, geneticists analysed nine purported yeti specimens together with additional bear material from the region. The alleged yeti items included bone, tooth, skin, hair and faecal samples gathered from locations across the Himalayas and Tibetan Plateau. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/11/171128230404.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedaily.com">[ScienceDaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Study ties DNA samples from purported Yetis to Asian bearsNov 28, 2017 — Royal Society B, analyzed nine &quot;Yeti&quot; specimens, inc...</span></span></span>

The variety of materials was important. Hair alone can be difficult to identify because it often contains degraded DNA. Bones and teeth can sometimes preserve genetic information more effectively, allowing researchers to compare sequences against large databases of known animals. By testing multiple specimen types, scientists reduced the chance that a single contaminated or poorly preserved sample would distort the overall picture. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/284/1868/20171804/78775/Evolutionary-history-of-enigmatic-bears-in-the" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royalsocietypublishing.org">[Royal Society Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royalsocietypublishing.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Society PublishingEvolutionary history of enigmatic bears in the Tibetan Plateau...Nov 29, 2017 — Recently, two purported yeti sam...</span></span></span>

## How Bear DNA Explained the Specimens

The clearest result emerged from the [2017 study]({{ '2017-study/' | relative_url }}) led by Charlotte Lindqvist and colleagues. Of nine purported yeti specimens, eight were identified as bears native to the Himalayan region. The remaining specimen proved to be from a dog. No sample required the existence of an unknown primate or other undiscovered large mammal. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/284/1868/20171804/78775/Evolutionary-history-of-enigmatic-bears-in-the" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royalsocietypublishing.org">[Royal Society Publishing+2ScienceDaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royalsocietypublishing.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Society PublishingEvolutionary history of enigmatic bears in the Tibetan Plateau...Nov 29, 2017 — Recently, two purported yeti sam...</span></span></span>

## The Earlier “Polar Bear” Surprise

Before the 2017 results, one set of samples briefly attracted international attention because they appeared to match an ancient polar bear lineage.

In 2014, geneticist Bryan Sykes and colleagues analysed hair samples attributed to various cryptids, including alleged yeti specimens. Two Himalayan hair samples—one from Ladakh and one from Bhutan—showed the closest similarity to a fossil polar bear sequence from the Pleistocene epoch. The finding generated speculation that an unusual bear population, perhaps related to polar bears or a hybrid lineage, might underlie some yeti reports. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/281/1789/20140161/77194/Genetic-analysis-of-hair-samples-attributed-to" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royalsocietypublishing.org">[Royal Society Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royalsocietypublishing.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Society PublishingGenetic analysis of hair samples attributed to yeti, bigfoot and...by BC Sykes · 2014 · Cited by 49 — Two Himala...</span></span></span>

However, the result was quickly scrutinised. Other researchers argued that the short DNA fragment used in the analysis was insufficient for such a conclusion and that sequence degradation or limited comparison data could explain the apparent match. The later Lindqvist study effectively resolved much of the controversy by using broader genetic datasets and additional specimens. ## What the Results Taught About Regional Bears

One of the most interesting outcomes of the yeti investigations had little to do with cryptozoology itself. The research provided new insight into poorly studied bear populations living in some of the world's most remote mountain environments.

Genetic analysis showed that [Himalayan brown bears]({{ 'brown-bears/' | relative_url }}) represent a distinctive lineage with a long evolutionary history. Their ancestors appear to have become isolated from other brown bear populations during ancient glacial periods, allowing them to follow a separate evolutionary path. Tibetan brown bears showed different relationships, linking them more closely to other Eurasian and North American brown bear populations. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/284/1868/20171804/78775/Evolutionary-history-of-enigmatic-bears-in-the" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royalsocietypublishing.org">[Royal Society Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royalsocietypublishing.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Society PublishingEvolutionary history of enigmatic bears in the Tibetan Plateau...Nov 29, 2017 — Recently, two purported yeti sam...</span></span></span>

## What Yeti Relic Testing Demonstrates About Cryptid Evidence

The yeti DNA investigations are among the clearest examples of how biological evidence can be evaluated in cryptozoology. Rather than relying on eyewitness accounts or ambiguous photographs, researchers examined physical specimens that could be independently tested.

The outcome was consistent across the strongest datasets: alleged yeti relics were overwhelmingly identified as known animals, especially regional bears. The studies therefore did not verify the existence of an unknown Himalayan ape. Instead, they demonstrated the value of careful sample collection, documented provenance and modern genetic analysis in determining what a specimen actually is. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/284/1868/20171804/78775/Evolutionary-history-of-enigmatic-bears-in-the" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royalsocietypublishing.org">[Royal Society Publishing+2University at Buffalo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royalsocietypublishing.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Society PublishingEvolutionary history of enigmatic bears in the Tibetan Plateau...Nov 29, 2017 — Recently, two purported yeti sam...</span></span></span>
