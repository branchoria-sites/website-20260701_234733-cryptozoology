## What eel size can and cannot explain

The strongest argument for the eel hypothesis is not that giant eels are common, but that even a moderately large eel can look surprisingly strange in dark water.

European eels have long, flexible bodies and swim with an undulating motion. Seen briefly at the surface, an eel can create the impression of a neck, hump or serpentine animal. Researchers and Loch Ness investigators have noted that some eyewitness descriptions—particularly those involving dark, elongated shapes—are broadly consistent with large eels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390620130_The_Loch_Ness_Monster_If_It%27s_Real_Could_It_Be_an_Eel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) The Loch Ness Monster: If It&#x27;s Real, Could It Be an Eel?The Monsters of Loch Ness. However, the theory runs into problems when size estimates are examined. A 2023 statistical analysis of eel lengths concluded that finding a European eel around one metre long in Loch Ness would be rare but plausible. Given the estimated eel population, such individuals could occasionally exist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/996475" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eurekalert.org">[EurekAlert!+2JMIRx Bio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eurekalert.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">news releases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EurekAlert!Unraveling the Loch Ness Monster&#x27;s eel connection24 Jul 2023 — The chances of encountering a 1-meter eel in Loch Ness, accordi...</span></span></span>

The same study found that the probability of encountering dramatically larger eels falls off rapidly. The author concluded that while unusually large eels might explain reports of relatively small unidentified creatures, they are a poor explanation for sightings involving enormous animals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/996475" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eurekalert.org">[EurekAlert!]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eurekalert.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">news releases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EurekAlert!Unraveling the Loch Ness Monster&#x27;s eel connection24 Jul 2023 — The chances of encountering a 1-meter eel in Loch Ness, accordi...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. The phrase “giant eel” can mean very different things to different people:

* A one-metre eel is unusual but biologically realistic.
* A two-metre eel would be extraordinary and unsupported by known records.
* Multi-metre monsters comparable to classic Nessie depictions would require sizes far beyond what evidence for European eels currently supports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://divernet.com/scuba-news/marine-biology/rise-fall-of-the-loch-ness-monster-eel/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: divernet.com">[Divernet+2EurekAlert!]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">divernet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rise fall of the loch ness monster eel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rise &amp; fall of the Loch Ness Monster eel27 Jul 2023 — The maximum length recorded for a European eel was 0.932m, Foxon found, lea...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_loch_ness_dna_39c301_giant_eel_nessie_the_ccc3d6-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Giant Eels illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_loch_ness_dna_39c301_giant_eel_nessie_the_ccc3d6-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_loch_ness_dna_39c301_giant_eel_nessie_the_ccc3d6-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

## A modest explanation rather than a monster

The appeal of the giant eel theory lies in its balance between scepticism and mystery. It does not require an [undiscovered species]({{'new-species/' | relative_url }}), a surviving prehistoric reptile or a hidden [breeding population]({{'missing-traces-2ce98c/' | relative_url }}) of giant animals. Instead, it starts with a creature known to inhabit the loch and asks whether unusual individuals, combined with difficult viewing conditions, could produce at least some of the reports.

Current evidence suggests that this is plausible for a subset of Nessie sightings. The abundant eel DNA found in Loch Ness gives the idea a stronger biological foundation than many rival theories. Yet the available evidence also suggests limits: realistic eel sizes may explain some observations, but they do not comfortably match the largest and most famous monster descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2019/sep/05/loch-ness-monster-could-be-a-giant-eel-say-scientists" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[Divernet+3The Guardian+3University of Otago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">loch ness monster could be a giant eel say scientists</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianLoch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists5 Sept 2019 — Loch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists ·...</span></span></span>
