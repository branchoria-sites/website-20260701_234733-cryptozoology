## Introduction

Environmental DNA (eDNA) does not make eyewitness lake-monster reports disappear, but it changes how they are interpreted. Before eDNA surveys, a sighting of an unidentified shape, wake or animal in a lake could be linked to a wide range of biological possibilities, including undiscovered large creatures. After eDNA sampling reveals which animals are actually leaving genetic traces in the water, many of those possibilities become much harder to defend. The result is not the end of eyewitness testimony; rather, sightings are filtered through a narrower set of biologically plausible explanations. In famous cases such as Loch Ness, eDNA has shifted discussion away from prehistoric-survivor theories and towards explanations involving known animals, optical effects and misidentifications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2019/sep/05/loch-ness-monster-could-be-a-giant-eel-say-scientists" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">loch ness monster could be a giant eel say scientists</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianLoch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists5 Sept 2019 — Loch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists ·...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_sightings_after_edna_18accb-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Sightings vs DNA illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_sightings_after_edna_18accb-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_sightings_after_edna_18accb-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## What Happens to Sightings After DNA?

The most important change introduced by eDNA is that sightings are no longer evaluated in isolation. A witness may still report a long neck, humps, a large wake or an animal-like shape, but investigators can compare those reports against an independently collected biological record of the lake.

In Loch Ness, the 2018 eDNA survey found genetic traces from thousands of organisms but did not find evidence supporting popular candidates such as large reptiles, sharks, catfish or [sturgeon]({{'sturgeon/' | relative_url }}). It did, however, detect abundant eel DNA. That finding did not prove that every sighting was an eel, but it changed the balance of probabilities. Explanations involving animals known to inhabit the loch remained viable, while explanations requiring an undetected population of large reptiles became much harder to reconcile with the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2019/sep/05/loch-ness-monster-could-be-a-giant-eel-say-scientists" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian+2Genome BC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">loch ness monster could be a giant eel say scientists</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianLoch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists5 Sept 2019 — Loch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists ·...</span></span></span>

As a result, eyewitness accounts increasingly function as descriptions of observed phenomena rather than direct evidence for a specific unknown species. The sighting remains real as an experience, but the biological interpretation becomes more constrained.

## Why Eyewitness Reports Still Matter Differently

eDNA cannot tell researchers what a witness saw at a particular moment. It cannot reconstruct weather [conditions]({{'conditions/' | relative_url }}), viewing angles, distance estimation errors or unusual animal behaviour. For that reason, eyewitness reports still provide information that DNA sampling cannot.

What changes is the role of the testimony.

Instead of asking, “Did someone see a monster?”, investigators increasingly ask:

* What physical event produced the observation?
* Which known species present in the lake could match the description?
* Could the sighting involve waves, [wakes]({{'wakes/' | relative_url }}), birds, floating objects or multiple animals?
* Does the report require a species that should have left detectable genetic traces?

This shift moves eyewitness evidence from the centre of the argument to part of a larger evidential framework. A dramatic report can still inspire investigation, but it no longer stands alone against biological surveys.

## Which Explanations Survive Biological Testing?

eDNA does not identify the exact cause of a sighting. Instead, it eliminates some explanations and leaves others standing.

### Explanations weakened by eDNA

Theories requiring large [populations]({{'populations/' | relative_url }}) of unknown reptiles, surviving plesiosaurs or other conspicuous animals become less persuasive when repeated [DNA surveys]({{'dna-survey/' | relative_url }}) fail to detect traces consistent with those groups. In Loch Ness, the absence of reptilian DNA was widely viewed as a serious challenge to the classic plesiosaur model of Nessie.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2019/sep/05/loch-ness-monster-could-be-a-giant-eel-say-scientists" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">loch ness monster could be a giant eel say scientists</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianLoch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists5 Sept 2019 — Loch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists ·...</span></span></span>

### Explanations that remain plausible

Some hypotheses survive because they involve animals already known to inhabit the lake.

The best-known example is the eel hypothesis. Abundant eel DNA demonstrates that eels are present. Since some historical reports describe elongated bodies, dark colouring and serpentine movement, proponents argue that unusually large eels could account for at least a subset of sightings. Researchers studying the idea have noted similarities between certain eyewitness descriptions and eel characteristics, although evidence for truly gigantic eels remains limited.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390620130_The_Loch_Ness_Monster_If_It%27s_Real_Could_It_Be_an_Eel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) The Loch Ness Monster: If It&#x27;s Real, Could It Be an Eel?The Monsters of Loch Ness. ## How DNA Changes Misidentification Debates

One of the most significant effects of eDNA is its influence on discussions of misidentification.

Historically, sceptics often proposed that [witnesses]({{'witnesses/' | relative_url }}) confused ordinary animals with extraordinary ones. Supporters of monster theories could respond that an unknown creature was equally possible because the lake had not been fully explored.

eDNA alters that balance. Once a survey provides a reasonably detailed inventory of local wildlife, investigators gain a stronger basis for comparing reports with known species. A dark shape at the surface is more likely to be interpreted in relation to animals confirmed by DNA evidence than to an entirely undocumented species.

This does not mean witnesses are dismissed. Instead, their observations are reclassified. A report may be treated as evidence that something unusual was seen, while the biological explanation is sought among organisms already known to inhabit the environment.

## How Folklore Adapts to Scientific Limits

Lake-monster traditions have proved remarkably resilient. eDNA has changed scientific discussions more than it has changed folklore.

Legends are not maintained solely by zoological claims. They are also sustained by local identity, tourism, storytelling and the human attraction to mystery. Even after the Loch Ness survey reduced support for several biological theories, public interest in Nessie remained strong and new sightings continued to be reported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: otago.ac.nz">[University of Otago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">otago.ac.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first edna study of loch ness points to something fishy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of OtagoFirst eDNA Study Of Loch Ness Points To Something Fishy5 Sept 2019 — The “monster-factor” has provided Professor Gemme...</span></span></span>

What tends to change is the content of the legend. Older versions often imagined a prehistoric survivor hidden in deep water. More recent discussions increasingly incorporate eels, unusual fish, optical illusions and environmental conditions alongside traditional monster imagery. ## A New Relationship Between Sightings and Science

The most lasting contribution of eDNA to lake-monster investigations may be methodological rather than biological. It changes the relationship between anecdote and evidence.

In earlier eras, eyewitness reports often drove the search for [hidden animals]({{'hidden-animals/' | relative_url }}). Today, DNA surveys provide an independent check on those interpretations. Sightings still matter because they record human experiences and sometimes point researchers towards interesting events. However, the range of explanations that can reasonably accompany those sightings becomes increasingly constrained by what the lake's genetic record reveals.

For cryptozoology, this represents a significant shift. eDNA does not settle every mystery, but it forces eyewitness accounts to coexist with ecological data.
