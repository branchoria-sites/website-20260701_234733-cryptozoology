## Introduction

Environmental DNA (eDNA) can only identify an organism by comparing recovered genetic fragments against reference sequences from organisms that have already been catalogued. This creates an important limitation for cryptozoological investigations: if a lake contains a genuinely unknown animal, there may be no exact sequence in any database to match it. In that situation, eDNA does not simply return a species name. Instead, the result may be assigned to a broader taxonomic group such as a genus, family, or order, or it may remain unclassified altogether. The strength of eDNA is therefore greatest when searching for known lineages, while its ability to recognise truly unknown creatures depends heavily on the completeness and quality of reference libraries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/13/13/1767" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Are Genetic Reference Libraries Sufficient for...by CL Jerde · 2021 · Cited by 30 — However, eDNA metabarcoding&#x27;s main weakness is t...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_edna_reference_datab_4abad4-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="DNA Databases illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_edna_reference_datab_4abad4-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_edna_reference_datab_4abad4-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## How DNA Reads Become Species Names

When researchers sequence DNA recovered from lake water, they generate thousands or millions of short genetic reads. Those reads are then compared with large reference collections such as GenBank and the Barcode of Life Data System (BOLD), which contain DNA barcodes from known organisms. BOLD alone now contains millions of barcode records representing more than a million species.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05696" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BOLD v4: A Centralized Bioinformatics Platform for DNA-based Biodiversity DataApril 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2024</span></span></span>

The assignment process is essentially a matching exercise. If a recovered sequence closely matches a known species in the reference database, software can assign that read to species level. If no exact match exists, the identification becomes less specific. A sequence might be recognised only as belonging to a particular genus, family, or higher taxonomic group. In some cases, researchers can conclude only that the DNA belongs somewhere within a broad branch of the tree of life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.naturemetrics.com/report-interpretation-guide" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naturemetrics.com">[naturemetrics.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naturemetrics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Report Interpretation GuideA species-level assignment will be given if reference databases contain an exact DNA match. For a lake-monster investigation, this distinction is crucial. Detecting DNA from a known eel, trout, or amphibian is relatively straightforward when reference sequences exist. Detecting an animal never previously sampled by science is much harder because the system has nothing definitive against which to compare the sequence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/13/13/1767" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Are Genetic Reference Libraries Sufficient for...by CL Jerde · 2021 · Cited by 30 — However, eDNA metabarcoding&#x27;s main weakness is t...</span></span></span>

## What Would a Truly Unknown Animal Look Like in the Data?

A genuinely undescribed lake-dwelling vertebrate would not necessarily appear in eDNA results as “unknown monster”. Instead, its DNA would likely be interpreted through its nearest known relatives.

Imagine a hypothetical animal closely related to eels but distinct enough to represent an undescribed species. Its DNA might produce results indicating membership within the eel lineage while failing to match any recognised eel species precisely. Researchers could therefore infer that the organism belongs somewhere within that group without being able to assign a formal species name.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.naturemetrics.com/report-interpretation-guide" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naturemetrics.com">[naturemetrics.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naturemetrics.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Report Interpretation GuideA species-level assignment will be given if reference databases contain an exact DNA match. ## What Broad Lineage Matches Can Still Reveal

Even when species-level identification fails, broad taxonomic matches can still provide useful evidence.

If repeated lake surveys consistently recovered DNA belonging to a vertebrate lineage not otherwise known from the ecosystem, researchers would have a specific biological signal worth investigating. The finding would not prove the existence of a cryptid, but it would narrow the search considerably. Instead of asking whether an unknown animal exists at all, investigators could focus on determining which branch of life the mystery organism belongs to.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12897059/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Central Fine-Grained Assignment of Unknown Marine e DNA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by S Villon · 2026 — In this study, we propose a deep learning-based approach designed to improve taxonomic assignment at higher level...</span></span></span>

This is one of the reasons eDNA remains valuable for lake-monster research despite database limitations. The method can potentially reveal:

* Whether detected DNA resembles fish, amphibians, reptiles, mammals, or another major group.
* Whether unusual sequences recur across locations and sampling periods.
* Whether unexplained signals cluster around a known evolutionary lineage.

## The Real Challenge for Future Lake-Monster Searches

The greatest obstacle for eDNA-based cryptozoology may not be collecting DNA from water but interpreting unusual sequences once they are found. Modern sequencing technology can recover enormous amounts of genetic information, yet the value of that information remains tied to the reference libraries available for comparison. Researchers continue to emphasise that database completeness, sequence quality, regional coverage, and taxonomic accuracy are among the most important factors determining what eDNA can reveal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/edn3.70080" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[Wiley Online Library+2Springer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryPersistent Gaps and Errors in Reference Databases...by A De Santiago · 2025 · Cited by 12 — High-throughput environm...</span></span></span>

As reference databases grow, the space for genuinely unexplained DNA signals becomes smaller. At the same time, any persistent sequence that cannot be linked to known organisms becomes more scientifically interesting.
