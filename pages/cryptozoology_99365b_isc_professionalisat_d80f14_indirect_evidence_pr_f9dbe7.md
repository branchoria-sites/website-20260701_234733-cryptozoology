## Introduction

The central methodological problem facing [cryptozoology]({{'cryptozoology/' | relative_url }}) was not a lack of reports. It was a lack of proof. For decades, investigators collected footprints, eyewitness accounts, photographs, casts, hairs, sounds, and folklore traditions suggesting the existence of animals unknown to science. The International Society of Cryptozoology (ISC), founded in 1982, attempted to treat such material as legitimate zoological evidence rather than dismissing it outright. Yet the society ran into a barrier that ultimately limited its acceptance within mainstream biology: the evidence that generated cryptozoological interest was usually indirect, whereas zoology generally requires direct, verifiable, and reproducible evidence before recognising a [new species]({{'new-species/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/288975639_A_Review_of_Cryptozoology_Towards_a_Scientific_Approach_to_the_Study_of_Hidden_Animals" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Review of Cryptozoology: Towards a Scientific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) A Review of Cryptozoology: Towards a Scientific...December 18, 2015 — Cryptozoology is still generally considered a no...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2015</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_isc_professionalisat_d80f14_indirect_evidence_pr_f9dbe7-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Proof Gap illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_isc_professionalisat_d80f14_indirect_evidence_pr_f9dbe7-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_isc_professionalisat_d80f14_indirect_evidence_pr_f9dbe7-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
This “[proof]({{'proof/' | relative_url }}) gap” was not merely a disagreement about belief. It reflected different evidentiary standards. Cryptozoologists often argued that clues should justify investigation. Zoologists generally agreed with that principle but maintained that investigation and acceptance were separate stages. [Tracks]({{'tracks/' | relative_url }}) might justify a search. Hair might justify laboratory testing. Testimony might identify locations worth surveying. None of these, by themselves, normally establish the existence of a new species.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03080188241233107" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sage Journals Heuvelmans the Heretic and Hidden Animals</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsHeuvelmans the Heretic and Hidden Animals - Floe Foxon...21 Mar 2024 — Cryptozoology seeks to study &#x27;hidden animals&#x27; by se...</span></span></span>

The ISC found itself caught between these positions. It wanted to professionalise the study of [hidden animals]({{'hidden-animals/' | relative_url }}) by collecting and evaluating evidence systematically. Yet most of the creatures that attracted public attention remained supported primarily by evidence that zoologists regarded as preliminary rather than decisive. This mismatch between investigative evidence and taxonomic proof became one of the most important obstacles to cryptozoology's scientific ambitions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/cl/article/view/41746/44352" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scholarworks.iu.edu">[scholarworks.iu.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scholarworks.iu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">of Pop Goes the Cryptid: The Evolution of Cryptozoology...by SA Hill · 2026 — The International Society of Cryptozoology (ISC) formed in...</span></span></span>

## What Counted as Indirect Cryptid Evidence?

Cryptozoological investigations typically relied on several recurring categories of evidence. None was inherently worthless. Indeed, zoologists routinely use similar clues in wildlife research. The problem was that such evidence rarely demonstrated the existence of an entirely unknown large animal beyond reasonable doubt.

### Eyewitness testimony

Witness reports formed the foundation of many famous cryptid traditions. Bigfoot sightings in North America, reports of the Yeti in the Himalayas, and alleged encounters with unknown lake creatures often depended heavily on human observation.

From a zoological perspective, testimony can provide useful leads but remains vulnerable to misidentification, memory distortion, expectation effects, poor viewing conditions, and cultural influences. Wildlife biology frequently uses observations to guide surveys, but the observation itself is not treated as proof that a new species exists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03080188241233107" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sage Journals Heuvelmans the Heretic and Hidden Animals</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsHeuvelmans the Heretic and Hidden Animals - Floe Foxon...21 Mar 2024 — Cryptozoology seeks to study &#x27;hidden animals&#x27; by se...</span></span></span>

Cryptozoologists such as Bernard Heuvelmans argued that recurring witness descriptions across different regions could indicate a biological reality behind legends. Critics responded that consistency alone does not establish existence because narratives can spread culturally and influence subsequent reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cryptozoologicalreferencelibrary.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/heuvelmans-1988.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cryptozoologicalreferencelibrary.wordpress.com">[Cryptozoological Reference Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cryptozoologicalreferencelibrary.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cryptozoological Reference Library Heuvelmans, B</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1988). ### Tracks and footprint casts

Footprints became especially important in Bigfoot investigations. Researchers collected casts showing unusually large tracks, dermal ridge patterns, and anatomical features that some investigators interpreted as evidence of an unknown primate.

Tracks are valuable ecological evidence because they indicate an animal's presence even when the animal itself is absent. However, they are also difficult to authenticate conclusively. Tracks can degrade, be misinterpreted, or be deliberately fabricated. Without an associated specimen, zoologists generally regard footprints as suggestive rather than definitive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tetzoo.com/blog/2021/4/23/comments-on-an-interview-with-jeff-meldrum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tetzoo.com">[Tetrapod Zoology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tetzoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">comments on an interview with jeff meldrum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hancock House, Surrey, B.C. &amp; Blaine, WA. ### Photographs and films

Images occupy a particularly controversial position in cryptozoology because some of the field's most famous cases revolve around photographs.

The famous 1967 [Patterson–Gimlin]({{'patty-film/' | relative_url }}) film, often cited in Bigfoot discussions, illustrates the problem. The footage has been analysed repeatedly, producing arguments both for and against authenticity. Yet decades of debate have not yielded scientific consensus. The film remains evidence that something appeared on camera; it has not become evidence accepted by zoology as demonstrating a new species.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/288975639_A_Review_of_Cryptozoology_Towards_a_Scientific_Approach_to_the_Study_of_Hidden_Animals" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Review of Cryptozoology: Towards a Scientific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) A Review of Cryptozoology: Towards a Scientific...December 18, 2015 — Cryptozoology is still generally considered a no...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2015</span></span></span>

Photography can document unusual observations, but images alone often lack the anatomical, genetic, and ecological information necessary to establish a new taxon. Ambiguity in scale, resolution, perspective, and context frequently prevents decisive conclusions.

### Hair, tissue, faeces, and other biological traces

Among indirect evidence categories, biological samples appeared to offer the greatest promise because they could potentially be tested scientifically.

Hair samples attributed to Bigfoot, Yeti, Almasty, Orang Pendek, and other alleged unknown primates were collected for decades. The development of modern genetic techniques created an opportunity to evaluate such claims directly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rspb.2014.0161" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: royalsocietypublishing.org">[Royal Society Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">royalsocietypublishing.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Royal Society PublishingGenetic analysis of hair samples attributed to yeti, bigfoot and...by BC Sykes · 2014 · Cited by 49 — Genetic an...</span></span></span>

Yet laboratory testing repeatedly demonstrated how difficult it was to convert cryptozoological clues into proof. When geneticist Bryan Sykes and colleagues conducted a systematic DNA analysis of purported anomalous primate hair samples, nearly all were identified as belonging to known animals. ## How Zoology Validates New Animals

The proof gap becomes clearer when examining how zoology actually confirms new species.

Contrary to a common misconception, scientists do not reject unknown animals because they are unusual. Zoology discovers thousands of new species regularly. What matters is the quality and reproducibility of evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/zoolinnean/article/202/4/zlae043/7664331" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicHow to describe a new species in zoology and avoid mistakesby MF Braby · 2024 · Cited by 60 — When designating a holotype, th...</span></span></span>

Traditionally, species descriptions rely upon physical specimens that can be examined by other researchers. The International Code of Zoological Nomenclature centres species names around type specimens, particularly holotypes, which serve as permanent reference points for scientific study.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: code.iczn.org">[code.iczn.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">code.iczn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Article 73</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Name-bearing types fixed in the original publication...A holotype is the single specimen upon which a new nominal species-group taxon is...</span></span></span>

A holotype allows future scientists to verify anatomical features, compare [specimens]({{'specimens/' | relative_url }}), test hypotheses, and reassess classifications. This process creates a durable chain of evidence rather than relying on interpretation alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://code.iczn.org/types-in-the-species-group/article-73-name-bearing-types-fixed-in-the-original-publication-holotypes-and-syntypes/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: code.iczn.org">[code.iczn.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">code.iczn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Article 73</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Name-bearing types fixed in the original publication...A holotype is the single specimen upon which a new nominal species-group taxon is...</span></span></span>

Modern taxonomy increasingly incorporates DNA sequencing, ecological information, imaging, and other data sources. Nevertheless, the underlying principle remains similar: claims should be independently testable and available for scrutiny by other researchers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10443861/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tightening the requirements for species diagnoses would help...by FE Rheindt · 2023 · Cited by 48 — Future species descriptions must...</span></span></span>

Interestingly, zoological nomenclature does not absolutely require a preserved specimen in every circumstance. Scholars have noted that the Code technically allows descriptions under exceptional conditions without one. However, taxonomists consistently emphasise that photographs or DNA sequences alone are generally weaker forms of evidence and should not replace specimens when specimens can reasonably be obtained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/800037/Attaching-Names-to-Biological-Species-1-.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: repository.naturalis.nl">[repository.naturalis.nl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">repository.naturalis.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Attaching Names to Biological Species: The Use and Value...March 10, 2021 — by R Sluys · 2021 · Cited by 30 — The International Code of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 10, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why the ISC Was Stuck in the Middle

The International Society of Cryptozoology attempted to occupy a difficult position between outright scepticism and unquestioning belief.

Its founders argued that hidden animals should be investigated scientifically. Bernard Heuvelmans explicitly insisted that cryptozoology concerned flesh-and-blood animals rather than supernatural entities. Roy Mackal and other ISC figures similarly sought to frame the discipline as a branch of zoological inquiry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cryptozoologicalreferencelibrary.files.wordpress.com/2020/08/heuvelmans-1982.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cryptozoologicalreferencelibrary.files.wordpress.com">[cryptozoologicalreferencelibrary.files.wordpress.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cryptozoologicalreferencelibrary.files.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Heuvelmans 1982.pdfCryptozoology is not an arcane or occult zoology, as some, in an attempt to discredit it, have suggested...</span></span></span>

In principle, this approach aligned with standard scientific practice. Many legitimate discoveries begin with fragmentary evidence. Tracks, local reports, unusual specimens, and indigenous knowledge have occasionally contributed to the discovery of previously undocumented animals. Cryptozoologists frequently cited such cases to justify continued investigation of controversial reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/20109683/A_Review_of_Cryptozoology_Towards_a_Scientific_Approach_to_the_Study_of_Hidden_Animals_" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Review of Cryptozoology: Towards a Scientific Approach...January 1, 2015 — This chapter proposes a scientifically acceptable d...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2015</span></span></span>

The problem was that the transition from clue to confirmation rarely occurred in major cryptid cases.

The ISC could publish analyses of reports, evaluate tracks, compare witness accounts, and discuss possible biological explanations. However, without specimens, repeatable observations, or genetic confirmation, its strongest cases remained permanently stuck at the level of possibility. Mainstream zoologists could acknowledge that the clues were interesting while simultaneously concluding that the evidence remained insufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/288975639_A_Review_of_Cryptozoology_Towards_a_Scientific_Approach_to_the_Study_of_Hidden_Animals" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Review of Cryptozoology: Towards a Scientific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) A Review of Cryptozoology: Towards a Scientific...December 18, 2015 — Cryptozoology is still generally considered a no...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2015</span></span></span>

This created a structural dilemma. If the society applied strict zoological standards, many celebrated cryptid cases failed to meet them. If it relaxed those standards, it risked losing scientific credibility. The resulting tension affected the field's reputation and complicated efforts to establish cryptozoology as a recognised scientific discipline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/288975639_A_Review_of_Cryptozoology_Towards_a_Scientific_Approach_to_the_Study_of_Hidden_Animals" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Review of Cryptozoology: Towards a Scientific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) A Review of Cryptozoology: Towards a Scientific...December 18, 2015 — Cryptozoology is still generally considered a no...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2015</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_isc_professionalisat_d80f14_indirect_evidence_pr_f9dbe7-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Proof Gap illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_isc_professionalisat_d80f14_indirect_evidence_pr_f9dbe7-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_isc_professionalisat_d80f14_indirect_evidence_pr_f9dbe7-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The Lasting Significance of the Proof Gap

The proof gap remains one of the most important concepts for understanding cryptozoology. It explains why many scientists do not dismiss tracks, testimony, photographs, or biological traces outright, yet still decline to accept the existence of the animals those clues supposedly represent.

Indirect evidence performs an important investigative function. It identifies questions worth asking and locations worth searching. Zoological proof serves a different function: it establishes that a proposed animal actually exists and can be studied, classified, and verified by others.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/zoolinnean/article/202/4/zlae043/7664331" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicHow to describe a new species in zoology and avoid mistakesby MF Braby · 2024 · Cited by 60 — When designating a holotype, th...</span></span></span>
