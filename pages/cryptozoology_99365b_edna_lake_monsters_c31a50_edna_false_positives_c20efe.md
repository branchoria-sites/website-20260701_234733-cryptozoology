## Introduction

Environmental DNA (eDNA) is often presented as a powerful new way to test lake-monster claims because it can detect genetic traces left behind by animals in the water. However, a surprising DNA match is not automatically evidence that an unusual creature lives in a lake. One of the most important lessons from eDNA research is that positive detections can arise from [contamination]({{'contamination/' | relative_url }}), transported genetic material, laboratory mistakes, or misinterpretation of what a DNA signal actually means. Researchers therefore distinguish between detecting DNA and proving that a living population is present. In cryptozoological investigations, where extraordinary claims may rest on very limited evidence, understanding false positives is as important as understanding successful detections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12595" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[BES Journals+2Wiley Online Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2041 210X.12595</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BES JournalsCritical considerations for the application of environmental...by CS Goldberg · 2016 · Cited by 1438 — Critical consideratio...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_edna_false_positives_c20efe-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="False Positives illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_edna_false_positives_c20efe-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_edna_false_positives_c20efe-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## Can Monster DNA Be Misleading?

A common misunderstanding is that eDNA works like a biological photograph. In reality, it detects fragments of genetic material that have entered the environment. Those fragments can reveal that DNA from a species reached a [location]({{'location/' | relative_url }}), but they do not always prove that a living animal is currently inhabiting the sampled water.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/what-is-environmental-dna-edna.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhm.ac.uk">[Natural History Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhm.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">If someone tested a sample of the water, these pieces of DNA could indicate the recent...Read more...</span></span></span>

Researchers studying eDNA often separate two different problems:

* A **false-positive test**, where contamination or analytical error creates an incorrect DNA detection.
* A **false-positive inference**, where the DNA is genuinely present in the sample but the conclusion that a living animal occupies the lake is incorrect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/edn3.194" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[Wiley Online Library+2PubMed Central]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Online Library What do you mean by false positive?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Darling - 2021by JA Darling · 2021 · Cited by 128 — The term false positive is often misused in eDNA research and natural resource manage...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters greatly for lake-monster investigations. Even if an unexpected DNA sequence appears in a sample, investigators must determine whether it reflects a resident animal, transported material, contamination, or a technical artefact before treating it as evidence for an unknown creature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12595" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[BES Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2041 210X.12595</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BES JournalsCritical considerations for the application of environmental...by CS Goldberg · 2016 · Cited by 1438 — Critical consideratio...</span></span></span>

## How Contamination Enters Samples

Environmental [DNA surveys]({{'dna-survey/' | relative_url }}) involve many stages: collecting water, filtering it, extracting DNA, amplifying genetic markers, sequencing them, and comparing the results with reference databases. Contamination can occur at almost any step.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12595" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[BES Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2041 210X.12595</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BES JournalsCritical considerations for the application of environmental...by CS Goldberg · 2016 · Cited by 1438 — Critical consideratio...</span></span></span>

### Laboratory contamination

DNA laboratories face a similar challenge. Modern genetic techniques are extremely sensitive, [meaning]({{'meaning/' | relative_url }}) tiny amounts of stray DNA can sometimes be amplified. Cross-contamination between samples, aerosolised DNA from previous experiments, or carry-over from positive controls can generate detections that appear genuine unless careful safeguards are in place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12595" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[BES Journals+2Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2041 210X.12595</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BES JournalsCritical considerations for the application of environmental...by CS Goldberg · 2016 · Cited by 1438 — Critical consideratio...</span></span></span>

Because cryptozoological claims often focus on rare or unexpected sequences, contamination can be particularly misleading. A trace signal that appears exciting may actually be the kind of low-level contamination that rigorous controls are designed to identify and eliminate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12595" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[BES Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2041 210X.12595</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BES JournalsCritical considerations for the application of environmental...by CS Goldberg · 2016 · Cited by 1438 — Critical consideratio...</span></span></span>

## Why DNA Can Travel From Elsewhere

Not all misleading detections result from mistakes. Sometimes the DNA is genuinely present in the sample but arrived from somewhere else.

Environmental DNA researchers refer to this as the problem of allochthonous, or externally transported, DNA. Genetic material can move through waterways, enter lakes from inflowing rivers, or arrive via animals that carry biological material between habitats.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12595" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[BES Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2041 210X.12595</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BES JournalsCritical considerations for the application of environmental...by CS Goldberg · 2016 · Cited by 1438 — Critical consideratio...</span></span></span>

Examples discussed in the scientific literature include:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Predator faeces containing DNA from prey species.
* Birds transporting biological material between water bodies.
* Water movement carrying DNA downstream from its original source.
* Human activities transporting organisms or biological material between locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210x.70328" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[BES Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2041 210x.70328</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ballast water, transport) or naturally...</span></span></span></div>

For lake-monster searches, this means that an unexpected sequence does not necessarily indicate a large unknown animal living in the lake itself. The DNA may have originated elsewhere and been transported into the sampling area. The rarer and more surprising the detection, the more carefully this possibility must be examined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12595" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[BES Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2041 210X.12595</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BES JournalsCritical considerations for the application of environmental...by CS Goldberg · 2016 · Cited by 1438 — Critical consideratio...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_edna_false_positives_c20efe-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="False Positives illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_edna_false_positives_c20efe-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_edna_false_positives_c20efe-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The Database Problem: When a Match Is Not What It Seems

Another route to false confidence comes from species identification itself.

After sequencing, researchers compare DNA fragments against reference databases. The quality of the identification depends on how complete those databases are and how distinctive the recovered DNA fragment is. Short fragments may match multiple related species, while some organisms remain poorly represented in reference collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12595" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[BES Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2041 210X.12595</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BES JournalsCritical considerations for the application of environmental...by CS Goldberg · 2016 · Cited by 1438 — Critical consideratio...</span></span></span>

In a cryptozoological context, an unusual sequence may sometimes reflect:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--example" markdown="1">

* An incomplete reference database.
* A degraded DNA fragment that cannot be identified precisely.
* Similarity between closely related species.

## What the Loch Ness Example Actually Shows

The Loch Ness eDNA survey illustrates both the strengths and limits of the method.

## What Checks Make a Claim Stronger?

Because contamination and misleading detections are well-known risks, researchers use several safeguards before accepting an unexpected result.

A stronger claim usually includes:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Multiple independent samples producing the same result.
* Negative controls showing no contamination.
* Replicate laboratory analyses.
* DNA detections across different locations and times.
* Genetic markers that uniquely identify the organism.
* Independent confirmation from additional methods where possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bvna.com/insight/confidence-your-environmental-dna-study" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bvna.com">[Wiley Online Library+3bvna+3BES Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bvna.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Confidence in Your Environmental DNA StudyNegative control samples should be included in the field and filtering steps to assess for...</span></span></span></div>

## Why Caution Matters More for Extraordinary Claims

Environmental DNA has become one of the most promising scientific tools available to cryptozoology because it can test biological claims directly rather than relying solely on sightings and photographs. Yet its sensitivity creates a paradox: the same technology that can detect rare organisms can also detect contamination, transported DNA, and analytical noise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12595" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[BES Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2041 210X.12595</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BES JournalsCritical considerations for the application of environmental...by CS Goldberg · 2016 · Cited by 1438 — Critical consideratio...</span></span></span>

As a result, the most convincing eDNA evidence for an unusual lake inhabitant would not be a single surprising sequence.
