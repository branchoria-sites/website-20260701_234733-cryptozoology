## Introduction

One of the most persistent misunderstandings in discussions of [cryptozoology]({{'cryptozoology/' | relative_url }}) is the assumption that a “cryptic species” is simply a scientific term for a cryptid. In biology, however, the word *cryptic* has a very specific meaning. A cryptic species is a real, existing species that closely resembles another species and is only recognised as distinct through careful scientific investigation. A cryptid, by contrast, is a proposed animal whose existence remains unconfirmed and is usually supported primarily by sightings, [folklore]({{'folklore/' | relative_url }}), anecdotes or ambiguous evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/nature-index/topics/l4/cryptic-species-diversity-and-taxonomy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cryptic Species Diversity and TaxonomyCryptic species are genetically distinct lineages that exhibit minimal or no diagnosable morp...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_undiscovered_species_607d5a_cryptic_species_conf_9f694a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Cryptic Species illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_undiscovered_species_607d5a_cryptic_species_conf_9f694a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_undiscovered_species_607d5a_cryptic_species_conf_9f694a-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
This distinction matters because discoveries of cryptic species are sometimes cited as evidence that famous cryptids might also exist. Yet the two phenomena involve fundamentally different kinds of uncertainty. Cryptic species are uncovered through [specimens]({{'specimens/' | relative_url }}), genetics, anatomy, behaviour and taxonomy. Cryptids remain hypothetical until comparable evidence exists.

## How DNA and Anatomy Reveal Hidden Lookalikes

Modern molecular techniques have transformed species identification. DNA barcoding, genome sequencing and multi-gene analyses allow researchers to compare populations at a level impossible for earlier generations of taxonomists. These methods frequently reveal deep genetic divisions among animals that look nearly identical.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/nature-index/topics/l4/cryptic-species-diversity-and-taxonomy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cryptic Species Diversity and TaxonomyCryptic species are genetically distinct lineages that exhibit minimal or no diagnosable morp...</span></span></span>

A famous example involves the Neotropical skipper butterfly *Astraptes fulgerator*. Once treated as a single species, DNA barcoding revealed that it actually represented at least ten distinct species that differed in genetics and ecology despite their similar appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC522015/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCTen species in one: DNA barcoding reveals cryptic</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by PDN Hebert · 2004 · Cited by 4975 — Ten species in one: DNA barcoding reveals cryptic species in the neotropical skipper butterfly...</span></span></span>

The process rarely relies on DNA alone. Researchers typically combine several lines of evidence:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Genetic differences between populations.
* Anatomical traits that become visible after detailed examination.
* Distinct mating behaviours or calls.
* Ecological specialisations.
* Evidence that populations do not exchange genes in nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10480071/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How we study cryptic species and their biological implicationsby CP Shin · 2023 · Cited by 53 — This study reviews how cryptic species...</span></span></span></div>

Giraffes provide a useful illustration. For many years they were treated as a single species with multiple subspecies. Genetic analyses later indicated that several giraffe lineages experience little or no gene flow and have been evolving separately for hundreds of thousands of years, leading many researchers to recognise four species rather than one. The animals were always known to science; the discovery concerned their true evolutionary relationships, not their existence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cell.com/fulltext/S0960-9822%2816%2930787-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cell.com">[Giraffe Conservation Foundation+3Cell+3Senckenberg Naturforschung]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cell.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Multi-locus Analyses Reveal Four Giraffe Species Instead...by J Fennessy · 2016 · Cited by 326 — Two previously recognized subspecie...</span></span></span>

Similar findings have occurred across insects, fish, reptiles, mammals and plants, demonstrating that hidden biodiversity often exists in plain sight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/hiding-in-plain-sight-cryptic-species-may-be-all-around-us" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicHiding in plain sight, &#x27;cryptic species&#x27; may be all around us14 Feb 2022 — Scientists have discovered cryptic fish, bu...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_undiscovered_species_607d5a_cryptic_species_conf_9f694a-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Cryptic Species illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_undiscovered_species_607d5a_cryptic_species_conf_9f694a-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_undiscovered_species_607d5a_cryptic_species_conf_9f694a-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why Cryptic Species Are Not Evidence for Cryptids

The confusion arises because both cryptic species and cryptids involve something previously unknown. Yet the unknown element is very different.

With cryptic species, scientists begin with physical organisms already collected, photographed, observed or preserved in museums. The question is whether those organisms belong to one species or several. With cryptids, the question is usually whether the organism exists at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/nature-index/topics/l4/cryptic-species-diversity-and-taxonomy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cryptic Species Diversity and TaxonomyCryptic species are genetically distinct lineages that exhibit minimal or no diagnosable morp...</span></span></span>

## Why the Term Is Misused in Cryptid Debates

The misuse often stems from a rhetorical shortcut. Someone notes that scientists continue discovering [new species]({{'new-species/' | relative_url }}) and then argues that famous cryptids could therefore be real. This skips over the crucial question of *what kind* of species are being discovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/hiding-in-plain-sight-cryptic-species-may-be-all-around-us" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicHiding in plain sight, &#x27;cryptic species&#x27; may be all around us14 Feb 2022 — Scientists have discovered cryptic fish, bu...</span></span></span>

Many newly recognised species are cryptic species uncovered through laboratory analysis, museum research or detailed field studies. They are not giant animals repeatedly observed by the public but somehow never documented conclusively. The discovery process usually involves refining existing knowledge rather than overturning it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/nature-index/topics/l4/cryptic-species-diversity-and-taxonomy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cryptic Species Diversity and TaxonomyCryptic species are genetically distinct lineages that exhibit minimal or no diagnosable morp...</span></span></span>

Another source of confusion is the similarity of the words *cryptic* and *cryptid*. In biology, *cryptic* refers to hidden identity. In cryptozoology, *cryptid* refers to an animal whose existence remains disputed. The terms sound related, but they describe different concepts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/nature-index/topics/l4/cryptic-species-diversity-and-taxonomy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cryptic Species Diversity and TaxonomyCryptic species are genetically distinct lineages that exhibit minimal or no diagnosable morp...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_undiscovered_species_607d5a_cryptic_species_conf_9f694a-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Cryptic Species illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_undiscovered_species_607d5a_cryptic_species_conf_9f694a-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_undiscovered_species_607d5a_cryptic_species_conf_9f694a-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
