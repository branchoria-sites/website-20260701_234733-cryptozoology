## Introduction

The silver-backed chevrotain (*Tragulus versicolor*) is one of the clearest modern examples of how a seemingly “lost” animal can move from rumour and scattered local reports back into documented zoological reality. Known only from a handful of specimens collected in Vietnam and lacking scientific confirmation since 1990, the tiny deer-like mammal spent nearly three decades in an uncertain category: not officially extinct, but effectively absent from modern field records. That changed in 2019 when researchers used [local knowledge]({{'local-knowledge/' | relative_url }}) and carefully placed camera traps to obtain the first photographs of the species alive in the wild. The rediscovery became a landmark case in conservation biology because it demonstrated how [hidden mammals]({{'hidden-mammals/' | relative_url }}) can persist undetected in pressured landscapes, while also showing that extraordinary claims require verifiable evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Camera-trap evidence that the silver-backed chevrotain...by A Nguyen · 2019 · Cited by 41 — Here, we provide evidence that the sil...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_hidden_mammals_33084e_chevrotain_camera_tr_fbd019-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Chevrotain illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_hidden_mammals_33084e_chevrotain_camera_tr_fbd019-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_hidden_mammals_33084e_chevrotain_camera_tr_fbd019-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## Why the species was considered lost

The silver-backed chevrotain is a small, shy forest ungulate often called a mouse-deer, although it is neither a mouse nor a true deer. Scientific knowledge of the species was remarkably thin for most of its history. It was first described in the early twentieth century from a small number of [specimens]({{'specimens/' | relative_url }}) collected in southern Vietnam. After that, records were extremely sparse. A further specimen obtained in 1990 provided the last confirmed scientific evidence of the animal's existence for decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.discoverwildlife.com/news/silver-backed-chevrotain-mouse-deer-vietnam" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discoverwildlife.com">[Discover Wildlife]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discoverwildlife.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Discover Wildlife&#x27;Lost&#x27; mouse-deer rediscovered in Vietnam</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discover WildlifeNov 12, 2019 — A fifth specimen was collected in 1990 from central Vietnam. ## How local interviews guided cameras

The rediscovery did not begin with technology. It began with people who lived and worked in the region.

Researchers interviewed local villagers, hunters and forest rangers across several areas of southern Vietnam. Rather than asking broad questions about unusual animals, the team focused on reports of a distinctive grey or silver-coloured chevrotain that could be distinguished from the more common lesser chevrotain known in the region. These interviews helped narrow the search to specific forest blocks where recent sightings were considered plausible. ## How camera traps found a lost tiny deer

Camera traps are motion-activated cameras that photograph animals without requiring researchers to be physically present. They are especially useful for elusive species that avoid humans or occur at low densities.

For the silver-backed chevrotain, camera traps solved several problems simultaneously. They could operate continuously, capture nocturnal activity, and document animals moving through dense vegetation where direct observation would be unlikely. The photographs obtained during the survey provided the first images ever taken of the species alive in its natural habitat.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Camera-trap evidence that the silver-backed chevrotain...by A Nguyen · 2019 · Cited by 41 — Here, we provide evidence that the sil...</span></span></span>

### What they did not prove

The photographs did not establish that the species was secure.

Researchers could not determine from the initial images alone:

* The total population size.
* Whether multiple healthy [populations]({{'populations/' | relative_url }}) existed.
* The full geographic range of the species.
* Long-term population trends.
* The degree of genetic diversity remaining.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Camera-trap evidence that the silver-backed chevrotain...by A Nguyen · 2019 · Cited by 41 — Here, we provide evidence that the sil...</span></span></span>
