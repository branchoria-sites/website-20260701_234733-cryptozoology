## Introduction

Among the many attempts to explain modern monster sightings, few are as well supported as the link between alleged chupacabras and coyotes suffering from severe mange. Across Texas, the American Southwest and parts of Latin America, numerous carcasses and sightings initially presented as evidence of a mysterious blood-sucking creature have later been identified as known canids whose appearance had been dramatically altered by disease. Wildlife biologists, veterinarians and genetic testing have repeatedly pointed toward the same explanation: animals that are recognisably coyotes, dogs or related canids once their DNA and anatomy are examined, but which looked startlingly unfamiliar while alive or shortly after death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/weird/el-chupacabra-just-a-coyote-with-mites-biologist/1905264/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nbcdfw.com">[NBC 5 Dallas-Fort Worth+2Mongabay News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nbcdfw.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">el chupacabra just a coyote with mites biologist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OConnor told Live Science photos of supposed chupacabras are actually of coyotes or dogs with sarcoptic mange infections.Read more...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_animal_misidentifica_c56a55_mange_coyotes_chupac_d60c23-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Mange Coyotes illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_animal_misidentifica_c56a55_mange_coyotes_chupac_d60c23-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_animal_misidentifica_c56a55_mange_coyotes_chupac_d60c23-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
The importance of this case within [cryptozoology]({{'cryptozoology/' | relative_url }}) is not simply that a monster claim was challenged. It demonstrates a specific mechanism by which a familiar animal can become a cryptid: disease changes appearance, behaviour and body condition so severely that observers no longer recognise the species they are looking at.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://agrilifetoday.tamu.edu/2024/10/15/unmasking-the-chupacabra/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: agrilifetoday.tamu.edu">[AgriLife Today]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">agrilifetoday.tamu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">unmasking the chupacabra</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AgriLife TodayFrom spooky lore to science fact: Unmasking the &#x27;chupacabra&#x27;15 Oct 2024 — Texas A&amp;M AgriLife expert provides scientific exp...</span></span></span>

## How Mange Changes a Coyote's Appearance

Mange is a skin disease commonly caused by microscopic mites that burrow into the skin. In canids, severe infestations can trigger intense itching, inflammation, hair loss and secondary infections. As the condition progresses, the animal may lose most of its coat, exposing thickened, darkened or grey skin beneath. The resulting appearance is radically different from the healthy, fur-covered coyote most people expect to see.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.mongabay.com/2010/10/mystery-of-the-chupacabra-monster-likely-solved/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.mongabay.com">[Mongabay News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.mongabay.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mongabay NewsMystery of the chupacabra monster likely solvedBiologist Barry OConnor says that most chupacabra sightings are probably link...</span></span></span>

Several features repeatedly described in [chupacabra]({{'chupacabra/' | relative_url }}) reports can emerge naturally from advanced mange:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Extensive hair loss creates the impression of a hairless, unknown species.
* Thickened skin can appear leathery, reptilian or scaled.
* Weight loss produces a gaunt body, prominent ribs and an enlarged-looking head.
* Remaining patches of fur can resemble spines or a ridge running down the back.
* A thin tail may appear rat-like rather than canine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://phys.org/news/2024-10-spooky-lore-science-fact-unmasking.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phys.org">[Phys.org+2AgriLife Today]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phys.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">From spooky lore to science fact: Unmasking the &#x27;...15 Oct 2024 — Further amplifying the ridge-like characteristics reported in chupacab...</span></span></span></div>

Texas A&M wildlife specialists have noted that one of the last places many infected canids lose fur is along the shoulder and back region. This surviving strip of hair can create the illusion of dorsal spikes, a feature commonly reported in later chupacabra descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://phys.org/news/2024-10-spooky-lore-science-fact-unmasking.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phys.org">[Phys.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phys.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">From spooky lore to science fact: Unmasking the &#x27;...15 Oct 2024 — Further amplifying the ridge-like characteristics reported in chupacab...</span></span></span>

The visual transformation can be so dramatic that even people familiar with local wildlife may struggle to identify the animal correctly, especially when the sighting is brief or occurs at night.

## Why Hairless Animals Feel Unnatural

The strength of the mange explanation is not only biological but psychological. Humans identify animals largely through familiar patterns: fur colour, body proportions, movement and facial features. When disease strips away those cues, recognition becomes much harder.

A healthy coyote fits a mental template. A severely mangy coyote often does not. Observers may see an animal with exposed skin, unusual posture, protruding bones and an unfamiliar silhouette. Instead of recognising a diseased coyote, the brain searches for another category that better matches the strange appearance. In regions where chupacabra stories are already known, the legendary creature becomes an obvious candidate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://agrilifetoday.tamu.edu/2024/10/15/unmasking-the-chupacabra/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: agrilifetoday.tamu.edu">[AgriLife Today]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">agrilifetoday.tamu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">unmasking the chupacabra</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AgriLife TodayFrom spooky lore to science fact: Unmasking the &#x27;chupacabra&#x27;15 Oct 2024 — Texas A&amp;M AgriLife expert provides scientific exp...</span></span></span>

Mange can also alter behaviour. Infected animals expend significant energy fighting disease and may become desperate for food. As their condition worsens, they may approach farms, livestock enclosures or residential areas more often than healthy coyotes would. Encounters therefore occur in places where people are least expecting to see a wild predator and most likely to interpret it as something unusual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.chipchick.com/2024/10/most-sightings-of-chupacabras-the-mythical-monsters-from-latin-american-legend-are-likely-sick-coyotes-in-the-late-stages-of-mange" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: chipchick.com">[Chip Chick]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">chipchick.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Chip ChickMost Sightings Of Chupacabras, The Mythical Monsters...25 Oct 2024 — When a coyote has late-stage mange, they become more desp...</span></span></span>

## How Carcasses and Photos Spread the Claim

Many of the most famous chupacabra episodes began not with sightings but with bodies. Throughout the 2000s, unusual-looking carcasses discovered in Texas generated extensive [media]({{'media/' | relative_url }}) coverage and public speculation. Animals found dead or shot after livestock attacks were photographed, displayed and circulated online before scientific examination occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcnews.com/Technology/chupacabras-mysterious-animals-spotted-texas/story?id=11165688" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcnews.com">[ABC News+2ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Chupacabras?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mysterious Animals Spotted in Texas&quot;From all indications, it seemed to be a chupacabra.&quot; &quot;The claws, toenails, nothing like I&#x27;ve seen on...</span></span></span>

A recurring pattern emerged. Initial reports described unknown creatures with hairless skin, elongated muzzles and strange proportions. Subsequent analysis often identified them as coyotes, dogs or canid hybrids affected by severe mange or other health problems. Texas wildlife officials repeatedly pointed toward diseased canids rather than [undiscovered species]({{'new-species/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.strangemag.com/recentadditions/chupacabracoyote.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: strangemag.com">[strangemag.com+2NBC 5 Dallas-Fort Worth]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">strangemag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">n that the Cuero Creature was either a disfigured coyote...Read more...</span></span></span>

Photographs amplify the effect because still images remove context. A close-up picture may hide familiar proportions, obscure scale and exaggerate unusual features. Once labelled as a chupacabra, the image is frequently shared as evidence of the legend, even after expert identification becomes available. The photograph's emotional impact often travels further than the later correction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcnews.com/Technology/chupacabras-mysterious-animals-spotted-texas/story?id=11165688" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcnews.com">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Chupacabras?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mysterious Animals Spotted in Texas&quot;From all indications, it seemed to be a chupacabra.&quot; &quot;The claws, toenails, nothing like I&#x27;ve seen on...</span></span></span>

This process helps explain why certain famous [specimens]({{'specimens/' | relative_url }}) continue to circulate online as mysteries despite repeated biological explanations.
