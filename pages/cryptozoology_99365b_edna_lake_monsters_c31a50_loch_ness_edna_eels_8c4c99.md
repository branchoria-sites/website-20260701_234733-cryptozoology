## Introduction

The Loch Ness environmental DNA (eDNA) survey became one of the most discussed scientific investigations in modern [cryptozoology]({{'cryptozoology/' | relative_url }}) because it addressed a simple question: if a large unknown animal lives in the loch, should it leave genetic traces behind? The project’s results did not reveal evidence for a prehistoric reptile, giant catfish, [sturgeon]({{'sturgeon/' | relative_url }}), shark, or any other commonly proposed “monster” candidate. Instead, researchers found eel DNA throughout the loch, leading to renewed interest in the idea that at least some Nessie sightings could involve unusually large eels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: otago.ac.nz">[University of Otago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">otago.ac.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first edna study of loch ness points to something fishy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DNA data obtained is that what people are seeing is a very large eel. “There is a very significant amount of eel DNA. Eels are very...Re...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_loch_ness_edna_eels_8c4c99-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Loch Ness e DNA illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_loch_ness_edna_eels_8c4c99-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_loch_ness_edna_eels_8c4c99-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
However, the findings are often misunderstood. The survey did not discover a giant eel, nor did it prove that [Nessie]({{'nessie/' | relative_url }}) is an eel. What it showed was that eels are abundant in Loch Ness and that the genetic evidence failed to support several alternative monster explanations. The giant eel hypothesis survived the test largely because the DNA data could not rule it out.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: otago.ac.nz">[University of Otago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">otago.ac.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first edna study of loch ness points to something fishy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DNA data obtained is that what people are seeing is a very large eel. “There is a very significant amount of eel DNA. ## Why eel DNA attracted attention

Eels stood out because their DNA appeared widely across the loch. According to Gemmell, eel DNA was found at virtually every sampling [location]({{'location/' | relative_url }}), indicating that European eels are common inhabitants of Loch Ness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: otago.ac.nz">[University of Otago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">otago.ac.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first edna study of loch ness points to something fishy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DNA data obtained is that what people are seeing is a very large eel. “There is a very significant amount of eel DNA. That result revived an older idea: perhaps some sightings involve unusually large specimens of the European eel (*Anguilla anguilla*). Eels can have long, dark, flexible bodies that may produce unusual silhouettes at the water surface. Under poor viewing conditions, an eel or group of eels could potentially appear larger or stranger than it really is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.illumina.com/content/illumina-marketing/amr/en_US/company/news-center/feature-articles/loch-ness-edna.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: illumina.com">[Illumina]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">illumina.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">loch ness edna</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">If there is a monster in Loch Ness, this geneticist will find itJan 10, 2025 — Eels were already known to be plentiful in the loc...</span></span></span>

The attraction of the eel hypothesis is that it relies on an animal already known to inhabit the loch rather than an undiscovered species. In terms of biological plausibility, it requires fewer assumptions than theories involving surviving prehistoric reptiles or unknown giant freshwater predators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: otago.ac.nz">[University of Otago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">otago.ac.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first edna study of loch ness points to something fishy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DNA data obtained is that what people are seeing is a very large eel. “There is a very significant amount of eel DNA. Eels are very...Re...</span></span></span>

This is why the survey’s public presentation frequently highlighted eels. They represented the one commonly discussed candidate that the DNA evidence could not eliminate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2019/sep/05/loch-ness-monster-could-be-a-giant-eel-say-scientists" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">loch ness monster could be a giant eel say scientists</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianLoch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists5 Sept 2019 — Loch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists ·...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_loch_ness_edna_eels_8c4c99-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Loch Ness e DNA illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_loch_ness_edna_eels_8c4c99-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_loch_ness_edna_eels_8c4c99-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why eel DNA does not prove a giant eel

The crucial limitation is that environmental DNA reveals presence, not body size. DNA fragments shed into water cannot determine whether they came from many ordinary eels or a few exceptionally large ones. The survey established that eels exist in Loch Ness; it did not establish how large they are.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: otago.ac.nz">[University of Otago+2Popular Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">otago.ac.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first edna study of loch ness points to something fishy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DNA data obtained is that what people are seeing is a very large eel. “There is a very significant amount of eel DNA. Eels are very...Re...</span></span></span>

Gemmell himself emphasised this distinction. The quantity of eel DNA suggested eels were abundant, but the data could not reveal whether any individuals reached extraordinary dimensions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: otago.ac.nz">[University of Otago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">otago.ac.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first edna study of loch ness points to something fishy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DNA data obtained is that what people are seeing is a very large eel. “There is a very significant amount of eel DNA. Eels are very...Re...</span></span></span>

This point is often lost in popular retellings. Headlines sometimes implied that scientists had found evidence for a giant eel. In reality, the survey merely left that possibility open while ruling out several competing ideas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2019/sep/05/loch-ness-monster-could-be-a-giant-eel-say-scientists" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">loch ness monster could be a giant eel say scientists</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianLoch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists5 Sept 2019 — Loch Ness monster could be a giant eel, say scientists ·...</span></span></span>

Another challenge is biological scaling. Even if unusually large eels occur occasionally, many famous Nessie reports describe creatures considerably larger than known European eels.
