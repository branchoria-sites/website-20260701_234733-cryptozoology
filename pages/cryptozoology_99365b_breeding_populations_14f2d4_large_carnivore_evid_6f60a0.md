## Introduction

Claims about hidden or disputed animals often focus on sightings: someone reports seeing a large ape, a mysterious lake creature, or an unexpected predator. Wildlife agencies, however, do not evaluate such claims primarily through witness testimony. Large-carnivore monitoring has developed formal evidence hierarchies that distinguish observations that can be independently verified from those that cannot. These systems provide a practical model for [cryptozoology]({{'cryptozoology/' | relative_url }}) because they deal with rare, elusive and sometimes controversial animals that are genuinely difficult to detect. The key lesson is simple: extraordinary claims about breeding [populations]({{'populations/' | relative_url }}) require evidence that can be checked, preserved and re-examined. In modern wildlife governance, DNA samples, carcasses, telemetry records and unambiguous images carry far more weight than even sincere eyewitness reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lciepub.nina.no/pdf/638988265540892975_LCIE_Monitoring%20Standards_10_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lciepub.nina.no">[lciepub.nina.no]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lciepub.nina.no</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Monitoring standards for large carnivores in Europe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 23, 2025 — C1: Hard evidence = hard fact i.e., evidence that unambiguously confirms the presence of a target species (live captur...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 23, 2025</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_breeding_populations_14f2d4_large_carnivore_evid_6f60a0-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Hard Evidence illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_breeding_populations_14f2d4_large_carnivore_evid_6f60a0-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_breeding_populations_14f2d4_large_carnivore_evid_6f60a0-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## Why Monitoring Systems Grade Evidence

Large carnivores such as wolves, lynx, bears and wolverines are often elusive, wide-ranging and active in remote landscapes. Because mistaken identifications are common, European monitoring programmes have developed standardised evidence categories rather than treating all reports equally. The best-known examples are the SCALP criteria (originally developed for lynx monitoring in the Alps) and the related standards used by the Large Carnivore Initiative for Europe (LCIE).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dbb-wolf.de/wolf-management//scalp-criteria" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dbb-wolf.de">[dbb-wolf.de+2lciepub.nina.no]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dbb-wolf.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SCALP CriteriaC1: clear evidence = hard facts that clearly verify the presence of the respective animal species (live capture, animal fou...</span></span></span>

These systems generally divide records into three broad levels:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Category 1 (C1): Hard facts or clear evidence.**<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.dbb-wolf.de/wolf-management//scalp-criteria" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dbb-wolf.de">[dbb-wolf.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dbb-wolf.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SCALP CriteriaC1: clear evidence = hard facts that clearly verify the presence of the respective animal species (live capture, animal fou...</span></span></span>
* **Category 2 (C2): Confirmed indications validated by trained experts.**
* **Category 3 (C3): Unconfirmed observations.**<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.kora.ch/en/projects/large-carnivore-monitoring/scalp-categories" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kora.ch">[kora.ch+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kora.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SCALP categoriesC1 – clear evidence: wolves found dead, photos and videos in which wolves are clearly recognisable, live captures and gen...</span></span></span></div>

The purpose is not to dismiss [witnesses]({{'witnesses/' | relative_url }}). Rather, it is to separate evidence that can independently demonstrate an animal's presence from evidence that depends heavily on interpretation. A photograph, DNA profile or recovered body can be examined repeatedly by different experts. A fleeting visual encounter usually cannot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lciepub.nina.no/pdf/638988265540892975_LCIE_Monitoring%20Standards_10_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lciepub.nina.no">[lciepub.nina.no]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lciepub.nina.no</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Monitoring standards for large carnivores in Europe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 23, 2025 — C1: Hard evidence = hard fact i.e., evidence that unambiguously confirms the presence of a target species (live captur...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 23, 2025</span></span></span>

This distinction matters when the claim involves a [breeding population]({{'missing-traces-2ce98c/' | relative_url }}). Conservation authorities may record thousands of public reports, but population estimates, management decisions and legal protections are generally built on verifiable evidence streams rather than sighting counts alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.kora.ch/en/projects/large-carnivore-monitoring/what-is-monitoring" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kora.ch">[kora.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kora.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on kora.ch.</span></span></span>

## Which Evidence Counts as a Hard Fact?

Under modern large-carnivore standards, the strongest evidence is evidence that directly establishes the identity of the animal without relying primarily on personal judgement.<div class="youtube-embed-container youtube-embed-fallback youtube-embed-link-only"><div class="youtube-embed-card"><div class="youtube-embed-link-panel"><span class="youtube-embed-link-kicker">YouTube</span><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yiv76hi-QsU" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Genomic tools for conservation of species: a practitioner&#x27;s guide - Part II</a></div><div class="youtube-embed-footer"><p class="youtube-embed-title"><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yiv76hi-QsU" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Genomic tools for conservation of species: a practitioner&#x27;s guide - Part II</a></p><p class="youtube-embed-actions"><a class="youtube-embed-watch-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yiv76hi-QsU" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yiv76hi-QsU">Open on YouTube</a></p></div></div></div>

### DNA Evidence

Genetic evidence has become one of the most important tools in carnivore monitoring. DNA can be obtained from hair, scat, saliva left on prey, blood, tissue or urine. Proper laboratory analysis can identify the species and, in many cases, the individual animal. This allows researchers to estimate population size, track dispersal and confirm reproduction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lifewolfalps.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/A5_-LWA_WAG_Monitoring-Standards-of-the-Wolf-alpine-population.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lifewolfalps.eu">[Life Wolfalps EU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lifewolfalps.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Life Wolfalps EUTHE INTEGRATED MONITORING OF THE WOLF ALPINE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The collection of genetic material (from scat, hair, blood, urine and saliva collected...Read more...</span></span></span>

For cryptozoological claims, this standard is significant because a genuinely unknown large mammal should eventually leave recoverable genetic material. A DNA sequence that clearly does not match known species would represent a major scientific discovery. By contrast, many alleged cryptid hair [samples]({{'samples/' | relative_url }}) and biological traces have ultimately been identified as known animals when subjected to genetic testing.

### Dead Animals and Physical Remains

A carcass is often treated as definitive evidence. Monitoring standards repeatedly classify a dead animal among the strongest forms of confirmation because researchers can inspect anatomy, collect tissue samples and preserve [specimens]({{'specimens/' | relative_url }}) for future study.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lciepub.nina.no/pdf/638988265540892975_LCIE_Monitoring%20Standards_10_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lciepub.nina.no">[lciepub.nina.no+2dbb-wolf.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lciepub.nina.no</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Monitoring standards for large carnivores in Europe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 23, 2025 — C1: Hard evidence = hard fact i.e., evidence that unambiguously confirms the presence of a target species (live captur...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 23, 2025</span></span></span>

This is one reason large-animal cryptid claims face a substantial burden of proof. Breeding populations experience mortality. Animals die from disease, predation, accidents, starvation and old age. Over long periods, populations normally produce bones, teeth, tissue and other physical remains. The absence of such material becomes increasingly difficult to explain as the claimed population size increases.<img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_breeding_populations_14f2d4_large_carnivore_evid_6f60a0-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Hard Evidence illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_breeding_populations_14f2d4_large_carnivore_evid_6f60a0-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_breeding_populations_14f2d4_large_carnivore_evid_6f60a0-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
### Telemetry and GPS Data

Telemetry refers to tracking animals through radio collars, GPS devices or similar systems. Because location data are automatically recorded and independently verifiable, telemetry records are commonly placed within the highest evidence category.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lciepub.nina.no/pdf/638988265540892975_LCIE_Monitoring%20Standards_10_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lciepub.nina.no">[lciepub.nina.no+2dbb-wolf.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lciepub.nina.no</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Monitoring standards for large carnivores in Europe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 23, 2025 — C1: Hard evidence = hard fact i.e., evidence that unambiguously confirms the presence of a target species (live captur...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 23, 2025</span></span></span>

Although telemetry is usually used after an animal has already been captured and identified, its inclusion in monitoring standards illustrates a broader principle: evidence becomes stronger when it can be objectively recorded and independently checked.

### High-Quality Photographs and Video

Not all images are equal. Monitoring systems typically recognise only photographs or videos in which the animal is clearly identifiable. Blurry silhouettes, distant shapes and ambiguous images are often downgraded or excluded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.kora.ch/en/projects/large-carnivore-monitoring/scalp-categories" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kora.ch">[kora.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kora.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SCALP categoriesC1 – clear evidence: wolves found dead, photos and videos in which wolves are clearly recognisable, live captures and gen...</span></span></span>

This point is especially relevant to cryptozoology. Many famous cryptid photographs remain controversial precisely because they lack the clarity needed for reliable species identification. Wildlife agencies routinely reject comparable imagery when assessing real carnivore records. The standard is not whether an image is intriguing, but whether experts can confidently determine what it shows.<div class="youtube-embed-container youtube-embed-fallback youtube-embed-link-only"><div class="youtube-embed-card"><div class="youtube-embed-link-panel"><span class="youtube-embed-link-kicker">YouTube</span><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=egPC7f5dRkI" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Wolf conservation and management under the EU Action Plan | Nicola Notaro</a></div><div class="youtube-embed-footer"><p class="youtube-embed-title"><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=egPC7f5dRkI" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Wolf conservation and management under the EU Action Plan | Nicola Notaro</a></p><p class="youtube-embed-actions"><a class="youtube-embed-watch-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=egPC7f5dRkI" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=egPC7f5dRkI">Open on YouTube</a></p></div></div></div>

## What Happens to Tracks, Scat and Kill Sites?

Many field signs occupy an intermediate category. ## Where Eyewitness Reports Fit in the Hierarchy

The governance lesson from large-carnivore monitoring is straightforward. A claim that a hidden population of large animals exists is not tested primarily by counting stories.
