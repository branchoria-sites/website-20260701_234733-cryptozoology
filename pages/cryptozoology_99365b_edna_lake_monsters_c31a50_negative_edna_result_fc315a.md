## Introduction

A negative environmental DNA (eDNA) result is often presented as evidence that a lake monster is not there. In reality, non-detection is more nuanced. eDNA is a powerful way to look for traces of life in water, and the absence of a DNA signal can substantially weaken some monster hypotheses. However, a negative result rarely proves absolute absence. Its [meaning]({{'meaning/' | relative_url }}) depends on how thoroughly the lake was sampled, how much DNA the animal would be expected to shed, how long DNA persists in the environment, and how likely the laboratory methods were to detect it if it were present. Scientific eDNA studies therefore treat non-detection as a probability statement rather than a simple yes-or-no verdict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0165273" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.plos.org">[PLOS+2PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.plos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Environmental DNA (eDNA) Detection Probability Is...by LS de Souza · 2016 · Cited by 261 — Detection probability is inferred through...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_negative_edna_result_fc315a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Negative Results illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_negative_edna_result_fc315a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_negative_edna_result_fc315a-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
For [cryptozoology]({{'cryptozoology/' | relative_url }}), this distinction matters. A well-designed eDNA survey may strongly challenge claims of a resident population of large unknown animals, while still leaving open the possibility of an exceptionally rare visitor, a transient animal, or a creature that was simply missed.

## Why Non-Detection Is Not Perfect Absence

Environmental [DNA surveys]({{'dna-survey/' | relative_url }}) are subject to the same basic problem as any wildlife survey: a species can exist without being detected. Ecologists refer to this as a false negative. In eDNA work, false negatives can occur at several stages. Water [samples]({{'samples/' | relative_url }}) may fail to capture DNA from the target organism, DNA may be too degraded or diluted to detect, or laboratory analysis may miss a genuine signal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://methodsblog.com/2017/08/17/false-positives-false-negatives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: methodsblog.com">[Methods Blog+2PLOS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">methodsblog.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">For instance, a water sample may fail to ...Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Methods BlogUncertainties in Species Occurrence Data: How to deal ...17 Aug 2017 — In fact, eDNA surveys present the added challenge that...</span></span></span>

This is why eDNA researchers frequently use repeated sampling, replicate laboratory tests and statistical occupancy models. These methods estimate the probability that a species would have been detected if it were actually present. The stronger that detection probability becomes, the more meaningful a negative result is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0165273" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.plos.org">[PLOS+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.plos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Environmental DNA (eDNA) Detection Probability Is...by LS de Souza · 2016 · Cited by 261 — Detection probability is inferred through...</span></span></span>

## When a Negative Result Is Strongest

The evidential value of missing DNA increases dramatically when the claim being tested implies a large, permanent biological presence.

Consider a hypothetical lake monster described as a breeding population of large aquatic animals living year-round in a lake. Such animals would be expected to shed skin cells, mucus, waste products and other biological material continuously. If researchers collect samples from multiple depths, locations and seasons and repeatedly fail to find any trace of the expected DNA, the monster hypothesis becomes increasingly difficult to sustain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nps.gov/articles/aps-19-1-6.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nps.gov">[National Park Service+2Biodiversity and Evolution]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceEnvironmental DNA: An Emerging Tool for Understanding...29 May 2024 — Environmental DNA (eDNA) can be used to deter...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

A negative result is especially persuasive when:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Sampling covers much of the habitat rather than a single location.
* Multiple independent samples are collected.
* Detection methods have already been shown to find rare species in similar environments.
* Known resident species are detected successfully, demonstrating that the survey worked.
* Statistical analysis indicates a high probability that a target species would have been found if present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-91166-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[PMC+3Nature+3PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Optimising sampling and analysis protocols in...by A Buxton · 2021 · Cited by 89 — Here we analyse a large qPCR based eDNA data se...</span></span></span></div>

In practice, this means eDNA is often strongest against claims of hidden [populations]({{'populations/' | relative_url }}) rather than against the mere possibility of a single unusual animal.<img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_negative_edna_result_fc315a-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Negative Results illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_negative_edna_result_fc315a-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_negative_edna_result_fc315a-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Loch Ness as a Case Study

The Loch Ness survey led by geneticist Neil Gemmell illustrates how scientists interpret a negative result. The project collected hundreds of samples from different locations and depths and identified thousands of DNA signatures from organisms living in the loch. Yet researchers found no evidence supporting popular theories involving surviving plesiosaurs, large sharks, sturgeon or giant catfish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: otago.ac.nz">[University of Otago+2Science Focus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">otago.ac.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first edna study of loch ness points to something fishy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We can&#x27;t find any evidence of sturgeon either,” Professor Gemmell says...</span></span></span>

Importantly, the researchers did not claim to have proved that no unknown animal could ever be present. Their conclusion was narrower and more scientifically defensible: the survey found no DNA evidence for several specific monster candidates despite extensive sampling. The absence of reptile DNA, in particular, strongly undermined the idea of a resident population of large prehistoric reptiles inhabiting the loch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oldsaltblog.com/using-edna-to-search-for-nessie-in-scotland-and-angel-sharks-off-corsica/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oldsaltblog.com">[oldsaltblog.com+2Science Focus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oldsaltblog.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">using edna to search for nessie in scotland and angel sharks off corsica</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Using eDNA to Search for Nessie in Scotland and Angel...2 Oct 2023 — Geneticist Neil Gemmell of Otago University, who led the survey, s...</span></span></span>

The Loch Ness work therefore demonstrates what negative eDNA results do best. They eliminate or weaken particular biological explanations rather than resolving every possible mystery.<div class="youtube-embed-container youtube-embed-fallback"><div class="youtube-embed-card"><div class="youtube-embed-frame"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/vHGUbLhCyQs" title="Loch Ness monster might just be a giant eel" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" allowfullscreen></iframe></div><div class="youtube-embed-footer"><p class="youtube-embed-title"><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vHGUbLhCyQs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Loch Ness monster might just be a giant eel</a></p><p class="youtube-embed-meta">Channel: KiniTV</p><p class="youtube-embed-actions"><a class="youtube-embed-watch-link" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vHGUbLhCyQs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vHGUbLhCyQs">Open on YouTube</a></p></div></div></div>

## How Rare Visitors Complicate the Answer

The greatest limitation of negative eDNA evidence appears when the claimed animal is extremely rare.

Imagine a large creature entering a lake only occasionally, spending little time near the sampled areas, or existing as a single individual rather than a population. Such an animal would leave far less DNA behind than a resident breeding population. In that situation, a survey could realistically miss it even if it existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://methodsblog.com/2017/08/17/false-positives-false-negatives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: methodsblog.com">[Methods Blog+2Springer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">methodsblog.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">For instance, a water sample may fail to ...Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Methods BlogUncertainties in Species Occurrence Data: How to deal ...17 Aug 2017 — In fact, eDNA surveys present the added challenge that...</span></span></span>

Environmental conditions also matter. DNA degrades over time and can be diluted, transported or concentrated by water movement. Detection therefore depends not only on the animal itself but on where, when and how sampling occurred. Studies of eDNA detection routinely account for these uncertainties because imperfect detection is a recognised feature of the method.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326272375_Environmental_DNA_reveals_quantitative_patterns_of_fish_biodiversity_in_large_rivers_despite_its_downstream_transportation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[Nature+3ResearchGate+3Biodiversity and Evolution]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Environmental DNA reveals quantitative patterns of...Comparisons with long-term electrofishing surveys demonstrated th...</span></span></span>

For cryptozoological claims, this creates an asymmetry. The more elusive and infrequent a creature is claimed to be, the harder it becomes for eDNA to rule it out completely. At the same time, those same claims become harder to reconcile with decades of sightings that supposedly indicate a substantial, recurring presence.<img src="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_negative_edna_result_fc315a-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Negative Results illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_negative_edna_result_fc315a-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/cryptozoology_99365b_edna_lake_monsters_c31a50_negative_edna_result_fc315a-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## What Missing DNA Really Tells Us

A negative eDNA result is best understood as a test of expectations. If a proposed creature should be abundant, resident and biologically active, repeated failure to detect its DNA can become strong evidence against that hypothesis.
