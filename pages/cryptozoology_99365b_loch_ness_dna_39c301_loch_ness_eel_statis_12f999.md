## Introduction

The giant eel hypothesis gained attention after the 2019 environmental DNA (eDNA) survey of Loch Ness found eel DNA throughout the loch while failing to find evidence for large reptiles, sharks, [sturgeon]({{ 'sturgeon/' | relative_url }}) or catfish. That result left European eels as one of the few ordinary animals that could not be ruled out as an explanation for at least some Nessie reports. However, the debate quickly shifted from biology to statistics. The key question became not whether eels live in Loch Ness—they clearly do—but whether they can plausibly reach the sizes required by monster sightings. Once researchers examined eel catch data and modelled how body sizes are distributed, [giant eels]({{ 'giant-eels/' | relative_url }}) stopped being merely a biological possibility and became a problem of probability. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: otago.ac.nz">[University of Otago]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">otago.ac.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first edna study of loch ness points to something fishy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of OtagoFirst eDNA Study Of Loch Ness Points To Something FishySep 5, 2019 — University of Otago geneticist, Professor Neil Ge...</span></span></span>

## What Eel Catch Data Can Estimate

Environmental DNA can show that eels are present, but it cannot reveal how large those eels are. To address the size question, researchers turned to measurements from thousands of European eels collected in Loch Ness and other European waters. By analysing real length distributions, they could estimate how likely unusually large individuals would be. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencefocus.com/news/loch-ness-monster-dna-study-reveals-plausible-explanation-for-sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencefocus.com">[Science Focus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencefocus.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">loch ness monster dna study reveals plausible explanation for sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science FocusLoch Ness Monster DNA study reveals &#x27;plausible&#x27;...Sep 5, 2019 — Professor Gemmell said: “There. Rather than asking whether a giant eel is theoretically possible, the study asked how often one should appear if eel growth follows observed patterns. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://xbio.jmir.org/2023/1/e49063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: xbio.jmir.org">[JMIRx Bio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">xbio.jmir.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">x BioThe Loch Ness Monster: If It&#x27;s Real, Could It Be an Eel?by F Foxon · 2023 · Cited by 6 — While the chance of finding a large eel...</span></span></span>

## The Difference Between Large and Monster-Sized

One reason the eel theory initially sounded attractive is that European eels already have an elongated body shape. At a distance, especially in rough water, a large eel surfacing briefly could potentially be misjudged as something much bigger. The statistical analysis does not reject that possibility. In fact, it suggests that unusually large eels may account for some reports of relatively small unidentified animals. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://xbio.jmir.org/2023/1/e49063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: xbio.jmir.org">[JMIRx Bio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">xbio.jmir.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">x BioThe Loch Ness Monster: If It&#x27;s Real, Could It Be an Eel?by F Foxon · 2023 · Cited by 6 — While the chance of finding a large eel...</span></span></span>

The problem emerges when “large” becomes “monster-sized”.

Foxon's analysis estimated that a 1-metre eel would be uncommon but plausible, with odds of roughly one in 50,000. Given the estimated number of fish in the loch, that still leaves room for occasional large [specimens]({{ 'specimens/' | relative_url }}). <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://xbio.jmir.org/2023/1/e49063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: xbio.jmir.org">[JMIRx Bio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">xbio.jmir.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">x BioThe Loch Ness Monster: If It&#x27;s Real, Could It Be an Eel?by F Foxon · 2023 · Cited by 6 — While the chance of finding a large eel...</span></span></span>

However, many famous [Nessie]({{ 'nessie/' | relative_url }}) descriptions involve animals several metres long. Some reports describe creatures around 6 metres or more. When researchers extrapolated from observed eel growth patterns, the probability of encountering a European eel of that magnitude collapsed to effectively zero. The study concluded that while large eels could explain some smaller sightings, they do not provide a credible explanation for reports of truly gigantic animals. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390620130_The_Loch_Ness_Monster_If_It%27s_Real_Could_It_Be_an_Eel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) The Loch Ness Monster: If It&#x27;s Real, Could It Be an Eel?Results The chances of finding a large eel in Loch Ness are aro...</span></span></span>

The distinction is crucial. A one-metre eel and a six-metre eel are not separated by a simple factor of six. In statistical terms, they belong to completely different regions of the probability curve. One lies at the extreme end of a known distribution; the other lies beyond what the distribution reasonably predicts. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://xbio.jmir.org/2023/1/e49063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: xbio.jmir.org">[JMIRx Bio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">xbio.jmir.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">x BioThe Loch Ness Monster: If It&#x27;s Real, Could It Be an Eel?by F Foxon · 2023 · Cited by 6 — While the chance of finding a large eel...</span></span></span>

## Why Probability Changes the Claim

Before statistical modelling entered the discussion, the giant eel theory often relied on a simple observation: eels are present in Loch Ness, and some animals occasionally grow larger than average. That argument leaves open almost any size.

Probability narrows the possibilities. Once researchers know the typical size distribution of European eels, they can estimate how many individuals would be expected at increasingly larger lengths. If a proposed size requires numbers that are effectively impossible given the loch's population, the hypothesis becomes much weaker. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://xbio.jmir.org/2023/1/e49063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: xbio.jmir.org">[JMIRx Bio]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">xbio.jmir.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">x BioThe Loch Ness Monster: If It&#x27;s Real, Could It Be an Eel?by F Foxon · 2023 · Cited by 6 — While the chance of finding a large eel...</span></span></span>

This is why the giant eel idea became a statistical problem rather than a purely biological one. The issue is not whether an eel can be unusually large. The issue is whether the population contains enough individuals for extraordinary sizes to appear at all. Catch data suggest there is some room for rare large eels, but not for the monster-scale animals often associated with Nessie [folklore]({{ 'folklore/' | relative_url }}). <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390620130_The_Loch_Ness_Monster_If_It%27s_Real_Could_It_Be_an_Eel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) The Loch Ness Monster: If It&#x27;s Real, Could It Be an Eel?Results The chances of finding a large eel in Loch Ness are aro...</span></span></span>

The eDNA survey indirectly reinforced this conclusion. Professor Neil Gemmell noted that abundant eel DNA meant large eels could not be dismissed outright, but the genetic data could not distinguish ordinary eels from hypothetical giants.
